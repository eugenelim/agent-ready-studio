## Outcome

Multidisciplinary product teams can move from strategic uncertainty through
research, experience design, architecture, delivery, release, and learning
using connected, reviewable work rather than disconnected documents and opaque
agent sessions.

## Opportunity

Teams need a shared environment that preserves why work exists, which inputs
produced it, what evidence supports it, what was decided, and what should
happen next. Today that context is split across repositories, documents,
trackers, research tools, design tools, and agent transcripts, and no single
surface reconstructs it.

## Boundary

- The product must remain useful without agents, AgentBundle, Git, provider
  credentials, or a connected repository. This guardrail is the vision's
  load-bearing constraint, not a phase-one convenience.
- This vision does not assert that any capability beneath it is shaped,
  approved, funded, or scheduled.
- Agent execution is an optional capability, never a prerequisite for the
  product's core promise.

## Assumptions

Captured from the shaping conversation. None carries customer, usage, market,
or technical validation.

- A connected artifact-and-decision model is more durable than a document set
  or a transcript archive for multidisciplinary product work.
- Teams will accept an explicit review-and-decision step as the price of
  traceability.
- The same kernel can serve strategy, research, experience, architecture,
  delivery, and release without fragmenting into separate products.

## Unresolved questions

1. Which discipline's work must be excellent first for the connected model to
   be worth adopting at all?
2. What is the smallest team and time horizon over which connected work beats
   ordinary documents plus a tracker?
3. Does "useful without agents" mean feature-complete without agents, or
   coherent-but-slower without agents?
4. How much of the vision depends on Agent-Ready Repo conventions that Studio
   does not own?
